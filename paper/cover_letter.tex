\noindent Dear Editor,

\medskip

We submit the manuscript ``Auditable repeated-service contracts for distribution-connected batteries: separating feeder limits from persistent recovery state'' for consideration in \emph{Applied Energy}.

The manuscript addresses a practical gap in flexibility assessment: a battery offer that is feasible for one activation is not necessarily feasible after a partially completed recovery. We define a sequence-level service--recovery contract and audit every interval through an isolated three-phase OpenDSS snapshot. The experiment includes a pre-registered full-rearm negative control. This control is essential because it distinguishes a genuine persistent SOC effect from a changing feeder scenario or a solver-state artifact. On 214 training, 71 calibration, and 80 held-out Ausgrid customer-days, a frozen \SI{140}{kW} offer reaches 96.25\% held-out event success. The full-rearm capacity remains at \SI{140}{kW} through six events, while the reserve-limited contract falls to \SI{40}{kW} by the sixth event.

The work is aligned with the journal's interest in energy-system flexibility, distributed storage, and distribution-network operation. It complements operating-envelope, recovery-aware bidding, and AC flexibility studies by providing a reproducible acceptance interface for repeated services. The package contains the corrected OpenDSS adapter, data manifests, chronological split, experiment scripts, tests, generated figures, and the manuscript source.
