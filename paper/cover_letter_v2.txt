Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, “Network-feasible recovery scheduling for repeated distribution-flexibility services: an AC–SOC benchmark on the IEEE 13-node feeder,” for Applied Energy.

The manuscript addresses a practical evaluation problem in distribution flexibility: a battery that can deliver one call may not be able to recover its SOC through the same feeder before a later call. We provide an auditable offline benchmark that couples an isolated OpenDSS AC snapshot audit with a chronological SOC-constrained recovery schedule. The study compares a full-horizon AC-bounded benchmark, fixed recovery, a myopic rule, and an energy-only counterfactual under the same profiles, feeder, locations, and terminal SOC condition.

The strict external-date evaluation retains 219 complete dates from the public Ausgrid 2012–2013 source. At one IEEE 13-node placement, full-horizon recovery and myopic recovery are identical; at the second placement, the full-horizon benchmark gains 2.433 kW (95% date-cluster CI 1.682–3.293). The energy-only schedule fails AC replay for every baseline-feasible unit at both placements. We report these findings as a location-dependent offline screening result, with explicit limitations on hindsight information, feeder scope, and the active-power battery surrogate.
