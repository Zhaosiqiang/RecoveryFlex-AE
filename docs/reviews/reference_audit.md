## What the citations support

* Evans et al. support aggregate storage flexibility with discharge/loss/recovery and recovery guarantees; they do **not** support a claim that SOC recovery is novel, nor feeder voltage/thermal coupling.
* Xiao et al. define security regulating capability for distribution systems with a duration criterion; they support the “security/duration limit” prior-art sentence, but the claim “no terminal SOC contract” must be checked against the full article and should be phrased conservatively as “does not formulate the repeated terminal-SOC contract used here.”
* Cheng et al. support dynamic feasible-operation-region aggregation and DSO/VPP coordination; do not describe it as hidden-scenario or AC-audited unless the paper explicitly does so.
* Karneluti et al. support day-ahead joint scheduling of positive/negative explicit demand response with multiple activations and recovery periods in a PV–BESS–hot-water energy hub. The manuscript may contrast the absence of an internal unbalanced feeder replay only after checking the article’s model; phrase as “the published case study is an energy-hub scheduling model rather than our three-phase feeder audit,” not as a universal statement about all possible extensions.
* Giraldo et al. support risk-aware flexibility areas for EV charging pools via AC stochastic OPF. They are useful to motivate risk-aware AC flexibility, but the manuscript should not imply that the paper studies a BESS contract.
* Bolfek & Capuder support practical flexibility assessment under unobservable distribution-network conditions. Avoid calling their object a “static nodal range” without reading the equations; say “an observability-aware flexibility assessment” and state the specific missing BESS sequence only as a comparison of problem scope.
* Lankeshwara et al. support validated dynamic operating envelopes-enabled demand response in LV networks. They are a closer DOE prior than the old matrix wording suggests; do not say “no dynamic operating envelope.” Say the project adds explicit BESS recovery/deadline composition and repeated-event auditing.
* Wang et al. support communication-dependent planning and dynamic storage reservation. This is directly adjacent to the telemetry ablation; do not claim that communication-aware storage reservation is absent from prior work.
* Zhu et al. support a duration-assessment / trajectory-similarity method. It is not evidence of a BESS terminal-SOC model unless the article explicitly says so; phrase the contrast as “trajectory-based duration assessment versus our explicit service–recovery state transition.”

## Critical identifiability correction: contraction under deterministic full re-arm

If the battery is returned to exactly the same terminal SOC after each event, feeder loads/PV/topology and operating limits are deterministic and unchanged, and there is no degradation/temperature state, then the event map is identical at every call. The admissible service set is invariant: (P_M^*=P_1^*) for every (M), so (C_M=1). An observed (C_2<1) therefore cannot be attributed to “sequence-level SOC recovery” alone under that experiment.

The current `scripts/run_ieee123_event_tree.py` deliberately changes the second recovery path (`load_scale*1.10`, `pv_fraction*0.90` when `k==1`) and uses different named scenario rows for successive delivery/recovery windows. That can produce a lower second-event capacity, but it is a **scenario-transition stress test**, not evidence that a fully re-armed battery contracts in a static environment. The paper should label it “trajectory/environment transition penalty” and include a static-profile negative control showing (C_M=1). If the intended novelty is genuine sequence contraction, add a state that is not reset (e.g., SOC reserve deficit, temperature/degradation, recovery-energy availability, or uncertain future network state) and state the mechanism explicitly. Otherwise remove any claim that the contraction proves a new recovery kernel.

## Matrix-only records requiring correction or removal

* `arXiv:2603.27161` is **Yujia Li, Alexandre Moreira, Miguel Heleno, “Time Window-Based Netload Range Cost Curves for Coordinated Transmission and Distribution Planning Under Uncertainty”** (submitted 28 March 2026), [arXiv:2603.27161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27161). The matrix shorthand “scenario-robust service and post-service rebound” is directionally correct, but “one service-window interface” and the implication that repeated service is absent need the paper’s exact definitions; do not use a blanket novelty claim against it.
* `SSRN 7356679` is **Chong Gao, Wenyan Huang, Jianwei Gao, Ziqi Lian, “Local Battery Flexibility Service Procurement in Active Distribution Networks with AC Power-Flow Validation and Cost-Recovery Assessment”** (2026), DOI `10.2139/ssrn.7356679`. The abstract reports AC validation, procurement, avoided-loss payment, and lifecycle cost-recovery assessment on an IEEE 33-bus feeder. It is a close prior for AC-audited local battery procurement and must be cited when discussing procurement/cost claims. Its abstract does not establish a repeated service–recovery viability kernel; that difference may be retained only as a narrow scope distinction.

These two records are not in `paper/references.bib`; add complete entries only after the authors decide whether they are needed in the final introduction. The matrix should link to the arXiv and SSRN records rather than retain untraceable shorthand.
