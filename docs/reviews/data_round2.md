# Data integrity review, round 2 (Ausgrid aggregation)

**Date:** 2026-10-03  
**Reviewer:** independent read-only audit  
**Scope:** `scripts/build_profile_bank_v2.py`, `scripts/build_external_year.py`, the three local Ausgrid half-hour files, and the frozen group manifest. No experiment output or source script was changed in this review.

## Decision

The development-year bank is internally consistent for the aggregation implemented in `build_profile_bank_v2.py`. The **existing external-year `.npz`/JSON outputs are not admissible for reported results**: they were generated by an earlier builder whose group denominator and incomplete-customer handling were not valid. Its expression

```text
gcg.mean(axis=0) + clg.mean(axis=0)
```

has a different denominator for the controlled-load channel whenever only some customers have a CL row. With the intended interpretation that a customer without a CL meter contributes zero, the group mean must use the fixed group denominator:

\[
\bar L_g(t) = \frac{1}{30}\sum_{i\in g}GC_i(t) + \frac{1}{30}\sum_{i\in g}CL_i(t),
\]

where the second sum is zero only for customers that have no CL channel by design. The old builder used for the existing external outputs instead computes `mean(GC)` over GC rows plus `mean(CL)` over CL-present rows. This inflates the CL contribution by approximately `30 / n_CL,g` and also changes the denominator when an external customer is absent for part of a year.

## Development bank (`build_profile_bank_v2.py`)

For 2010–11, all 300 customers have complete GC and GG rows for all 365 dates. There are 139 customers with a CL row for every date and 161 customers with no CL channel. The script first reindexes every channel to the complete customer–date Cartesian product, fills absent CL rows with zero, forms `(GC + CL) / 0.5` per customer, and then averages each fixed group. This is the denominator-aligned operation for the development year. The resulting split is 219 train, 73 calibration, and 73 test dates under the strict chronological rule.

The development file does not contain a partial CL customer: CL is either complete for all 365 dates or absent. Consequently, the current development result does not expose the external builder's CL-present denominator error.

## External builder: measured denominator bias

The following read-only calculation reproduced the current expression and compared it with the fixed-30-customer denominator. For each available group-day and each half-hour interval, the difference was computed in kW after the `/0.5` conversion.

| Source | Mean absolute difference (kW) | Median absolute difference | 95th percentile | Maximum | Mean relative difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2010–11 | 0.121526 | 0.030533 | 0.582333 | 2.177083 | 14.58% |
| 2011–12 | 0.118590 | 0.033950 | 0.557351 | 1.892000 | 14.73% |
| 2012–13 | 0.113527 | 0.034259 | 0.525082 | 1.936733 | 14.50% |

The 2010–11 discrepancy is entirely due to CL-present rows: each group has between 8 and 17 CL customers (`[17, 14, 13, 15, 8, 15, 16, 15, 11, 15]`). The current formula is always greater than or equal to the fixed-denominator formula. The apparent percentage can be large at intervals where the true group load is close to zero; therefore both absolute and relative values are reported here.

This is a data construction error, not a statistical uncertainty. It affects every external policy schedule that consumes the old `.npz` files and can change network feasibility or the reported procurement frontier. A concurrent working-tree revision of `build_external_year.py` now contains a strict customer-level mask and fixed-denominator aggregation, but it has not yet been independently tested in this review; the existing `.npz`/JSON outputs still have the old 365/366-day manifests and must be treated as audit-only until rebuilt and verified against the repaired source.

## External completeness and customer coverage

The 2011–12 file has complete GC and GG for all customers and dates. Customer 27 has only 196 CL dates (the remaining CL rows are absent); all other CL-present customers have 366 dates. The builder treats this partial channel as present on the dates where it exists and applies the CL-present mean, so the denominator varies by date.

The 2012–13 file has a more serious gap. Customer 2 has only 284 dates for **each** of GC, GG, and CL; the missing period is 2012-10-12 through 2012-12-31 (81 dates). On those 81 dates, the fixed group containing customer 2 has 29 available GC and GG rows rather than 30. The old builder checked only that a group had at least one GC/GG row and therefore accepted these dates, averaging over 29 rows. It did not record the reduced denominator. CL has 138 customers in total; 11 have partial CL coverage (`2:284`, `68:300`, `95:318`, `161:306`, `187:307`, `248:320`, `272:300`, `284:300`, `289:305`, `293:317`, `294:307`) and 162 have no CL rows. These facts make the 2012–13 source a useful stress case for missingness handling, but the old output cannot be described as a clean complete-customer aggregate.

As a sensitivity benchmark, requiring every GC/GG customer and every customer with a CL channel to have one row on a retained date would retain 365/365 days in 2010–11, 196/366 in 2011–12, and 219/365 in 2012–13. These losses are a consequence of the source's partial coverage, not a reason to relax the denominator rule after seeing the service results.

## Holdout provenance statement

The 2012–13 archive was downloaded, extracted, inspected, and used to build and run development external checks during this project. The 2011–12 file was also previously inspected and used in an earlier check. Neither year should be called an **untouched** external holdout in a manuscript. A defensible statement is that they are calendar-year external datasets evaluated under a frozen protocol after the development design; a genuinely untouched claim would require a new source/year whose contents and outcomes were not inspected during method development.

## Required repair before scientific use

1. Build customer-level arrays indexed by the frozen 30 IDs for every date. Require complete GC and GG for every customer in a retained day (or explicitly publish and propagate a customer-day validity mask). Do not accept a group merely because `shape[0] > 0`.
2. Distinguish a structural no-CL customer (no CL channel over the source year) from a CL customer with a partial outage. Structural no-CL can contribute zero; partial CL must be marked invalid or handled by a predeclared, sensitivity-tested imputation rule. It must not be silently divided by the number of CL-present rows.
3. Aggregate with the fixed group denominator after the customer-level validity decision: `sum(GC)/30 + sum(CL)/30`, then convert half-hour energy to average kW.
4. Write a manifest containing source hash, category row/customer counts, retained-day mask, per-group available-customer counts, and the exact missingness rule. Rebuild all external `.npz` files and rerun the complete network experiment.
5. Report a strict-complete sensitivity alongside the primary rule if the 2012–13 calendar year loses dates. Do not select the rule or year based on which yields a larger service frontier.

The working-tree builder now appears to implement items 1–3. A spot check of the newly written strict 2012–13 array against direct customer-level recomputation on three retained dates and two groups gave a maximum 48-point error of 0.0 kW, but this is not a substitute for a clean regeneration and unit tests. The output has 219 retained dates in three non-contiguous blocks, so its manifest and statistical code must preserve the retained-date list rather than infer a continuous year. Until that verification and a fresh external run are complete, the existing full-year numerical frontiers should remain outside the manuscript and submission package.
